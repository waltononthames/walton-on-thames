Hersham Green is what gives the village its identity. At around 3.4 acres, it sits at the centre of Hersham, faced by a shopping parade, a scatter of period cottages and Victorian villas, and the pond that draws ducks and the occasional heron. For a village so close to Walton-on-Thames, the green gives Hersham a genuinely rural feel that Walton's own town centre does not have.

## The green today

The green is bordered by the Barley Mow, a pub that has stood beside it for generations, and by a run of period cottages and Victorian villas that give the green its settled, established character. It is the natural place to start any visit to the village: a genuine village green, complete with pond, in a location that is still only a short walk from Hersham railway station and about a mile from Walton town centre along Queen's Road.

What makes the green work as a public space is precisely that it has stayed a working green rather than becoming a museum piece. Dog walkers cross it on ordinary weekday mornings, families use it at weekends, and the pond is close enough to the pub and the parade that a walk around it naturally becomes part of a longer visit rather than a destination on its own. It is a small, unforced kind of civic space, and Hersham is fortunate to have kept it intact while the village around it has grown.

## Events through the year

The green hosts regular events across the year, from village fetes to seasonal gatherings, and functions as Hersham's default public space in the way a town square would elsewhere. Current listings for anything taking place on the green can be found on our [what's on page](/whats-on/).

## The village hall story

Hersham's village hall was built beside the green in 1885, and enlarged in 1892 to accommodate the Working Men's Club, a sign of how quickly the village's civic life grew once Hersham became a parish in its own right in 1851. The hall's presence beside the green, rather than tucked away elsewhere in the village, tells you something about how central this open space has always been to Hersham's sense of itself.

## The shopping parade

The parade of shops facing the green carries Hersham's everyday retail and hospitality, the kind of small, functioning high street that many larger places have lost. It sits alongside the wider food and drink scene covered in our [Hersham food and drink guide](/hersham/food-and-drink/), and gives the village a genuine centre rather than simply a green with houses around it.

## Practical notes

Hersham Green is easily reached on foot from Walton town centre via Queen's Road, around a mile, or from Hersham railway station, roughly a ten-minute walk. There is no dedicated car park at the green itself; on-street parking nearby is limited, so arriving on foot, by bus or by train is usually the simplest option. The green is unfenced and open at all times, with the pond as its natural focal point in any season.
