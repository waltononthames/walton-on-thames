The Watermans Arms is a traditional Hersham pub on Queens Road, serving food and drink alongside straightforward, good-value accommodation, within easy walking distance of Hersham village green, the Barley Mow, and Hersham railway station.

The building carries two names across its frontage, which is worth knowing before you arrive: **Watermans Arms** over the bar on the left, and **The Lodge** over the rooms on the right, with a central panel reading "Eat, Drink, Sleep". Both are the same premises. The pub side advertises pool, darts and televised sport.

This is a practical base for visitors who want to be in the heart of Hersham village rather than Walton town centre: useful for exploring the quieter, greener parts of the borough on foot.

**A punk landmark.** A green plaque on the front wall of the pub commemorates Sham 69, who formed in Hersham in 1975 and took the village's name into the UK top ten with "Hersham Boys". The plaque lists that single alongside "Hurry Up Harry" and "If the Kids Are United". [Read the full story of Sham 69 →](/hersham/sham-69/)

**Getting around:** Hersham railway station is a 10-minute walk, with South Western Railway services to London Waterloo and connecting buses to Walton-on-Thames. Rooms and tables are booked by telephone.
