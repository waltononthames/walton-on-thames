**Not an email.** A Talk page post, made from Darren's own account, with the connection disclosed. **Do not edit the article, and do not add our links to it.** Wikipedia's conflict of interest guidance is explicit that an interested party proposes on Talk and lets uninvolved editors decide.

**The strong part of this is not our site.** The article's external links are genuinely out of date, and saying so is a service whether or not anything of ours is ever cited.

**Current state, checked 6 September 2026:** the External links section offers "Hersham Community Organisation", pointing at a 2016 Wayback snapshot of thehershamhub.co.uk, and "Hersham Residents Association website" at hershamresidents.info. The Residents Association merged into the Hersham Community Group, whose constitution was revised on 1 October 2025, and the Hersham Village Society's separate registration was voluntarily deregistered on 30 June 2025 per Electoral Commission record PP1874.

> **External links are out of date, and a note on the drill plough sentence**
>
> Declaring an interest first: I run walton-on-thames.org, which covers Hersham, so I am not going to edit this article. Posting here instead.
>
> Two things, one of which has nothing to do with me.
>
> **1. The external links are stale.** "Hersham Community Organisation" points at a 2016 Internet Archive capture of thehershamhub.co.uk, and "Hersham Residents Association website" points at an organisation that no longer exists separately. The Residents Association and the Hersham Village Society merged to form the Hersham Community Group, which publishes at hershamcommunitygroup.co.uk and whose constitution was revised on 1 October 2025. The Electoral Commission's register (PP1874) records the Village Society as voluntarily deregistered on 30 June 2025. Replacing the two dead entries with the current organisation would fix the section regardless of anything else here.
>
> **2. The drill plough sentence in Notable events.** "King George III visited Weylands farm in Hersham where he saw the first drill plough" carries no citation. I could not trace it to any source. Jethro Tull had built a drill plough by about 1733 and George III acceded in 1760, so "the first drill plough" cannot be right as written even if the visit happened. The article's history section leans on George Greenwood's *Hersham in Surrey* elsewhere, which seems the obvious place to check whether the visit is documented at all. Until someone can, the sentence probably needs a citation, qualification or removal.
>
> I have written the same thing up on my own site, so treat that as disclosure rather than a suggestion to cite it. Editors may find some of the sourcing there useful for other parts of the article, particularly the Waitrose acquisition of the shopping centre in February 2026, but that is entirely for others to judge.
