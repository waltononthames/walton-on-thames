# Scope of this brief

This brief applies specifically to the **historical, heritage and long-form research content** published on Walton-on-Thames.org.

It governs pages and projects concerning subjects such as:

- local history;
- historic streets and road names;
- buildings and demolished sites;
- estates and land ownership;
- notable residents;
- businesses and industries of the past;
- churches and religious history;
- military and wartime history;
- archaeology;
- transport history;
- hospitals, schools and public institutions;
- historic events;
- local government history;
- biographies;
- historic maps and photographs;
- conservation and architectural heritage.

Examples include articles about Sidney Road, Church Street, Walton Lodge, Cottimore, Walton Cottage Hospital, the Birds Eye headquarters, the Cecil Hepworth film studios and the history of Walton Bridge.

This brief does **not** apply automatically to every part of Walton-on-Thames.org.

Practical and frequently updated services require different editorial and technical approaches. These include:

- current event listings;
- live calendars and iCalendar feeds;
- restaurant, pub and takeaway directories;
- business listings;
- transport information;
- parking information;
- opening hours;
- current public services;
- current visitor information;
- commercial venue pages;
- temporary notices and news updates.

These sections must still be accurate, useful, well structured and properly maintained. However, they do not require the full historical research workflow, claim-level evidence register, archival investigation or Harvard APA referencing described in this document unless they contain substantive historical material.

Where a page combines current information with historical content, apply the appropriate standard to each part.

For example:

- Current event dates should be verified against official event sources.
- A historical account of the venue should follow the historical research and referencing standards in this brief.
- Current opening hours should not be presented using academic citations.
- A claim about when a building opened or who designed it should be historically sourced.

Claude Code must therefore identify the content type before editing or creating a page.

---

# The historical-content vision

The objective for the historical and heritage section of Walton-on-Thames.org is to create the most authoritative, accurate and comprehensive digital record of Walton-on-Thames and its surrounding communities.

For this part of the site, every significant page should be capable of becoming:

- Google's preferred result;
- an AI assistant's preferred citation;
- a trusted source for local residents;
- a useful reference for historians, journalists and researchers;
- a permanent contribution to the documented history of the area.

This ambition applies to historical and heritage content. It should not force simple listings, calendars or current-information pages into an unnecessarily academic format.

# Different content types require different workflows

Claude Code should recognise at least four broad content classes.

## 1. Historical and heritage articles

Apply the complete research and evidence framework in this document.

Typical requirements include:

- research dossier;
- source register;
- evidence log;
- Harvard APA citations;
- references;
- historical transparency statement;
- image provenance;
- distinction between fact and interpretation;
- knowledge-graph entities;
- long-term archival maintainability.

## 2. Current information and service pages

Prioritise:

- current accuracy;
- official sources;
- clear user journeys;
- dates and opening times;
- location information;
- accessibility;
- contact and booking links;
- regular update processes;
- structured data appropriate to the page type.

Do not add academic referencing merely for consistency with history articles.

## 3. Event listings and calendars

Prioritise:

- verified event titles;
- correct dates and times;
- venue details;
- booking links;
- cancellation and change handling;
- timezone accuracy;
- comparison with previous feeds;
- safe retention of the previous valid calendar when source validation fails.

Historical context may be included where genuinely useful, but it should remain secondary to accurate event information.

## 4. Hybrid pages

Some pages combine practical information with historical interpretation.

Examples include:

- a visitor page for a historic building;
- a theatre page containing both current events and venue history;
- a road page containing current transport information and historical research;
- a business page discussing both present services and the history of its premises.

For hybrid pages, use separate sourcing methods for separate content blocks.

Current facts should be verified against current official sources. Historical claims should follow the full historical evidence standard.

---

# Governing principle

Do not apply one editorial method indiscriminately across the whole site.

The historical section should operate as a rigorous digital archive.

The current-information section should operate as a reliable and regularly maintained local service.

Both should be authoritative, but they achieve authority in different ways.
